## Timing and modeling decisions

- The 202 LIGHT minutes schedule 27 flying-fish groups and 26 bird dives. After applying stage boundaries and the open-water exclusion, 26 groups have at least one visible glide and 22 dives reach the water. A new stage can interrupt an event. At night the flock has three birds.
- The school uses one instanced mesh with up to 216 fish. The daylight and night scenes use the same animal positions. REM adds grain and blue-green points to the wake, bow wave, and water beside the companion at night. The flying fish uses a 0.45 m model for a 0.30 m reference length.
- The deck graph and boat exporter share the same socket coordinates. The captain walks 1.2 m per ambient unit. The route can reverse on its current edge at a stage boundary. A short stage can interrupt the walk or the binocular observation. The synthetic eight-minute awakening gives the captain enough time to reach the windlass. The anchor follows the previously approved lowering sequence while the captain walks forward.
- The predeparture line work starts before the session to allow the quay traverse at walking speed. At the island, the captain handles both lines at the boarding gate, crosses the gangway, waits on the T-head, and boards before the lateral departure. The ten-minute stop allows a short walk toward shore. The captain reaches the helm during the return passage.
- The smaller companions use a closer voyage framing to meet the screen-size threshold at the specified model sizes. The whale uses a wider frame. The sea-life GLB contains bow-ride and leap clips. The renderer samples the same motion curves at replay time. The single instanced pod shares geometry across poses.
